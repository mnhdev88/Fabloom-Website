How to Care for Pure Silk Fabric at Home

Pure silk is one of nature's most exquisite fibres — lustrous, breathable, temperature-regulating, and extraordinarily luxurious against the skin. But this very delicacy means that silk demands a level of care that synthetic or even cotton fabrics do not. Whether you've invested in a length of authentic Bhagalpur handloom silk, a Katan silk saree, or a digitally printed silk dupatta, the principles of good silk care remain the same.

This comprehensive guide — compiled by our expert artisans and textile specialists at Fabloom — will walk you through everything you need to know about washing, drying, ironing, storing, and refreshing your silk fabrics at home.

1. Washing Pure Silk: Always Hand-Wash

The golden rule of silk care is simple: never machine-wash pure silk. The agitation of a washing machine — even on a delicate cycle — can stretch, distort, and permanently damage silk fibres. Hand-washing is the only safe method for regular cleaning.

Use cold water (20–30°C) and a very small amount of mild, pH-neutral liquid detergent or a specialist silk wash. Baby shampoo works well in a pinch. Avoid anything with bleach, enzymes, or optical brighteners — these will strip the natural lustre from silk over time.

Submerge the fabric gently and swish it softly in the water for no more than 3–5 minutes. Never scrub, wring, or twist silk. Rinse twice in clean cold water to remove all soap residue, as any remaining detergent can dull the sheen.

Pro Tip from Our Artisans Add a tablespoon of white vinegar to the final rinse water. This neutralises any soap residue and helps restore the natural shine and softness of the silk fibres.

2. Drying Silk the Right Way

After washing, do not wring or twist silk — this will distort the weave structure permanently. Instead, lift the fabric carefully from the water and place it flat on a clean, dry towel. Roll the towel up gently with the silk inside to absorb excess moisture, then carefully unroll it.

Hang silk to dry in a well-ventilated spot out of direct sunlight. Direct sun causes silk to yellow and can fade dyes dramatically over time. If drying indoors, ensure good airflow. Never use a tumble dryer — the heat will damage and shrink silk irreparably.

3. Ironing Silk: Cool and Gentle

Silk wrinkles easily, but ironing is straightforward if done correctly. Set your iron to the lowest temperature setting (the "silk" setting if available). Always iron silk while it is slightly damp — not completely dry — and place a clean cotton cloth between the iron and the silk surface. Never iron silk when it is bone dry, as this can create shine marks.

Iron on the reverse (wrong) side of the fabric. Avoid steam settings unless your iron has a very gentle steam function, as water droplets can leave water marks on some silk types.

4. Storing Silk Properly

Good storage is just as important as careful washing. Store silk garments and fabric lengths in a cool, dry, dark place with good airflow. Avoid plastic bags — these trap moisture and can cause yellowing. Use breathable cotton garment bags or muslin wrapping instead.

If folding silk, refold it periodically along different lines to prevent permanent crease marks from forming. For silk sarees and long fabric lengths, rolling rather than folding is preferable.

Keep silk away from direct contact with cedar blocks, mothballs, or strong perfumes — silk fibres are sensitive to chemicals and strong odours. Use lavender sachets as a natural moth deterrent instead.

5. Dealing with Stains

Act quickly on stains — the sooner you treat them, the better. Blot (never rub) the stain gently with a clean white cloth. For food or liquid stains, cold water and a tiny amount of mild detergent applied with a soft touch usually suffice. For oil-based stains, a very small amount of talcum powder left on the stain for a few hours can help absorb the oil before washing.

For stubborn or complex stains — ink, dye, or set-in grease — we recommend taking the silk to a professional dry cleaner who specialises in delicate fabrics. Always mention to the cleaner that the fabric is pure silk, particularly if it is block-printed or digitally printed, so they can use appropriate solvents.

6. When to Dry Clean

Heavily embellished silk (with zari, embroidery, or structured weaves like Katan silk) is best dry-cleaned. The same applies to large silk sarees that are difficult to hand-wash evenly. For everyday Bhagalpur silk or linen-silk blends, hand-washing as described above is perfectly safe and preferable to dry cleaning, which uses solvents that can, over time, affect the natural softness of the fibre.

Quick Silk Care Checklist

  • Always hand-wash in cold water with a mild detergent
  • Never wring or twist — press gently to remove moisture
  • Dry in shade — never in direct sunlight
  • Iron on low heat with a pressing cloth, on the reverse side
  • Store in breathable cotton bags, away from plastic and chemicals
  • Blot stains immediately — never rub

With proper care, pure Bhagalpur silk can retain its lustre, softness, and beauty for decades. It is one of the most durable natural fibres when treated correctly — a testament to the extraordinary craftsmanship woven into every thread.
